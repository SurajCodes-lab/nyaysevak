## PART III — Why These Pages Can Compete

Google penalises templated local pages — the kind where a competitor swaps the city name into the same paragraph fifty times. Those pages do not rank, and increasingly they attract manual action.

Our defence is that **each city has genuinely different law applying to it**, and every page says so explicitly. This is the substantive work of the cycle, and it is what a competitor cannot cheaply copy.

| City | The distinguishing legal fact |
|------|------------------------------|
| **Jodhpur** | The **principal seat** of the Rajasthan High Court — Jaipur is only a bench. Writs from the western districts are filed here, and the pages name which districts and why. |
| **Kota & Ajmer** | Fall under the **Jaipur Bench**, not the Jodhpur principal seat. Stated explicitly, because filing at the wrong seat costs a litigant months. |
| **Ajmer** | Seat of the **Board of Revenue** — Rajasthan's apex revenue court, hearing appeals from every district in the state. Also the state public service commission and education boards. |
| **Udaipur** | Much of the division is a **Fifth Schedule Scheduled Area**, where the law restricts transfer of tribal land and a completed sale can be undone years later. |
| **Kota** | India's coaching capital — **fee-refund claims**, hostel tenancy, institutional liability, and guardianship for minors whose parents live in another state. No other Indian city generates this. |
| **Surat & Rajkot** | **Gujarat is a prohibition state**, with serious consequences after the 2017 amendments. Rajkot adds **Saurashtra's separate land-tenure laws**, which do not apply in the rest of Gujarat. |
| **Vadodara** | A large organised-sector industrial workforce, so **workplace-safety and pollution prosecutions** that name senior plant staff personally, alongside standing orders and contract-labour work. |
| **Gandhinagar** | State capital plus **GIFT City**, India's only operational international financial centre, with its own dedicated regulator — a category of work that exists nowhere else in India. |
| **Greater Noida** | Differentiated from Noida by the **allotting authority** rather than the courthouse — both are heard at the same complex, and the pages say so rather than implying otherwise. |

### The detail competitors get wrong

Court jurisdiction and tribunal jurisdiction do not follow the same map. Jodhpur's High Court work is heard at Jodhpur, but its company and insolvency work goes to **Jaipur**. Ghaziabad and Greater Noida businesses trade inside the NCR but file company matters at **Allahabad**, not Delhi.

Getting these right is a small, checkable signal of genuine local competence — and it is exactly what a prospective client notices when comparing two firms' websites.

## PART IV — Technical Corrections

### A gap that was keeping pages out of Google's index

The site's court pages — one of the largest groups of pages on the site — had **no internal links pointing to them from anywhere**, only the sitemap. Search engines treat a page that nothing links to as a page that does not matter, and this is the single most common reason pages sit in Google's *"Discovered — currently not indexed"* bucket rather than appearing in results.

Every city page now links to its state's court directory. This should improve indexation of that whole group over the coming weeks.

### Two pages competing for the same city

Ahmedabad's page listed Gandhinagar as one of its own localities and claimed its courthouse. That was reasonable while Gandhinagar had no page of its own. Now that it does, the two would have competed for the same searches and split the signal between them. Ahmedabad's page has been corrected.

## PART V — The Structured-Data Correction

**This is the most consequential change of the cycle, and it is worth understanding.**

Structured data is the machine-readable summary every page sends to Google. It is a public statement about your business, exactly like the visible text.

Every city page was telling Google that NyaySevak **has a physical office in that city**, complete with map coordinates. Across 22 cities, the site was declaring 22 separate business premises — while your organisation details state a single base.

Two independent problems, pointing the same way:

1. **It contradicted your compliance position.** You have a team across these cities but deliberately do not showcase them, offices, or fees, because of Bar Council advertising rules. The visible pages follow that discipline carefully. The structured data did not — and it is just as public.

2. **It worked against your Google Business Profile.** Google cross-checks what a website claims against the verified profile. One verified location against twenty-two asserted ones is a conflict, and a conflict *weakens* the connection between your site and your profile rather than building it.

**What it says now:** a service covering each city, provided by one business, with the area served named explicitly. Nothing about local relevance is lost — the city coverage was always carried by that "area served" statement, and local map rankings come from the Google Business Profile, never from this markup. What has gone is a claim we could not stand behind.

## PART VI — The Google Business Profile

Every report since Week 18 has identified the Google Business Profile as the single highest-leverage action available to you. It creates a verified real-world entity that separates you from the similarly named competitor you compete with on brand searches, unlocks Maps visibility, and enables genuine reviews — the trust signal your competitors have and you do not.

**It is currently suspended, and this outranks everything else in this report.**

To diagnose it we need two things only you can supply: **the suspension email** (a soft suspension leaves the listing visible but removes your access; a hard suspension removes it from Maps and Search entirely — the recovery routes differ), and **the profile's current business name, address, and primary category.**

### Most likely causes, in order

1. **The address.** Google requires a business that serves customers at their location to *hide* its address, and any address it does display must be a real place staffed during the hours you publish. A virtual office, co-working desk, registered-agent address, or a visible home address is the most common suspension trigger for legal businesses of this shape.
2. **Keywords in the business name.** Anything beyond plain "NyaySevak" is the second most common trigger.
3. **A recent edit.** Changing the name, address, category, or website triggers an automated re-review — and that is usually the moment a borderline listing is suspended.

**Context worth knowing:** legal services is one of Google's high-scrutiny categories, alongside locksmiths and addiction treatment. Enforcement is more aggressive and the threshold lower than in ordinary industries.

**To appeal**, you will need business registration (GST or incorporation certificate), a utility bill or lease for the address, and photographs of signage if you have a physical office.
